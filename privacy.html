Privacy Policy

Your library stays yours.

Papyrus is local-first. Your PDFs, notes, annotations, evidence, search index, and library database stay on your Mac, iPad, or iPhone unless you choose to export, share, look up metadata, turn on iCloud Sync, or configure WebDAV sync.

Last updated: November 18, 2026

Summary

  • No Papyrus account is required.
  • Apple and Google sign-in are optional. Your default device library stays independent of sign-in.
  • Papyrus does not run developer-operated advertising, tracking, or analytics services. Optional Google sign-in uses Google's SDK and the data practices described below.
  • Your library is stored locally by default.
  • Network requests happen only for features you choose, such as metadata lookup, optional OpenAlex suggestions in Similar Papers, iCloud Sync, WebDAV sync, a web search you start from selected text, or an AI provider you set up with your own API key.
  • Apple Intelligence features — Key Findings, Explain Connections, Explain Figure, and concept tagging — run with Apple's on-device model. The text and images they work on do not leave your device for them, and Papyrus does not use Private Cloud Compute or any hosted AI service for them unless you choose one, as described below. Optional Private Cloud Compute metadata refinement is separate, off by default, asks for consent first, and is not in the versions currently on the App Store.
  • You can optionally use OpenAI or Anthropic as your AI provider with your own API key. It is off by default and asks for consent first. Requests go directly from your device to that company; Papyrus's developer receives nothing.
  • The optional Safari extension runs only when you invoke it and hands captured papers to the app on your device.

Local Data

Papyrus stores research data on your device, including the library database, imported PDFs, linked-file bookmarks, annotations, Markdown notes, project evidence, reading progress, local search indexes, preferences, and rolling local backups.

Credentials that should not be stored in plain text, such as WebDAV passwords, are stored through the system keychain where supported by the platform. iCloud Sync uses the Apple ID and iCloud account already configured on your device; Papyrus does not ask for or store your iCloud password.

Optional OpenAI and Anthropic API keys are stored only in this device's Keychain. They do not sync, and they are not included in backups, sync packages, the library database, preferences, or logs.

Sign-In and Account Deletion

If you choose Sign in with Apple, Apple provides an app-scoped identifier and, when you consent, a name and email address, which may be an Apple private relay address. Papyrus stores this profile in the device-only Keychain, not on a Papyrus server. Your default device library and its own sync configuration are preserved. If you have a separate Apple library, signing in may reopen it according to your saved choice or when the current device library is empty, or offer it through recovery. This does not change the system's iCloud account; iCloud sync uses that system account independently of this optional profile.

If you choose Sign in with Google, Google's official SDK authenticates your Google Account. Papyrus stores the provider-specific user identifier, name, and email in the device-only Keychain; the SDK manages its authorization session and credentials through the system Keychain. Email addresses do not merge identities. Google sign-in keeps your independent device library and iCloud sync settings unchanged. It requests basic sign-in/profile access, not Google Drive access.

If you have a separate Apple library, it retains its own data and sync settings. You can open it explicitly using its Apple identity; libraries are not automatically merged.

To request deletion, open Settings → Account → Sign-In → Delete Papyrus Account. You can cancel the destructive confirmation before deletion starts. For Apple sign-in, deletion removes your Papyrus sign-in profile and any separate library owned by that Apple identity, including its managed files, database, app-managed backups, library preferences, credentials, and associated account-scoped iCloud and configured WebDAV data. Your independent default device library and its sync configuration are kept. Linked original files outside Papyrus are not deleted. If deletion cannot finish, Papyrus keeps the request pending and offers a retry; completion is shown only after the required steps succeed. Deleting the currently open separate Apple library requires reopening Papyrus to use your independent device library.

To prevent another device from recreating a deleted account library through sync, Papyrus retains minimal deletion controls in the associated private iCloud and configured WebDAV storage. These include deletion markers, otherwise empty iCloud record zones with a lifecycle guard in each zone, and a WebDAV marker. They identify the opaque library namespace and its deleted status; they contain no name, email address, authorization token, or library content. These controls remain after account data is removed for the purpose of preventing that library from being synced again.

Deleting Apple sign-in account data does not automatically revoke Apple authorization. After completion, open your Apple Account, choose Sign-In & Security → Sign in with Apple, select Papyrus, and stop using Sign in with Apple. See Apple's revocation instructions for the device and website steps. Signing out is not account deletion or authorization revocation.

For Google sign-in, deletion removes your Papyrus Google profile and uses the official SDK's disconnect operation to revoke Papyrus's Google authorization. A failed disconnect remains pending for retry and is not shown as complete; if the session is missing, sign in with the same Google Account to finish. Your independent device library, Google Account, and Google Drive files are not deleted. Ordinary Sign Out clears this device's session without claiming to revoke access. Google may retain authentication and service records under its own policies.

Network Use

Papyrus does not upload your library to a Papyrus cloud. The app may contact outside services only when a feature needs a network request.

Metadata lookup

When you ask Papyrus to enrich metadata, it may send public identifiers or search terms, such as DOI, arXiv ID, ISBN, PMID, title, or author names, to services including Crossref, arXiv, Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, OpenLibrary, NCBI / PubMed / PMC identifier services, Zenodo, and doi.org.

Similar Papers

Similar papers from your own library are ranked on your device; nothing is sent to rank them. Online suggestions of related papers you do not have yet, shown under From OpenAlex, are a separate option. It is off by default, asks before it turns on, and is available only while online metadata lookup is enabled. When it is on, viewing a paper's Similar Papers sends that paper's DOI to OpenAlex (api.openalex.org), along with the contact email you entered for metadata lookup, if any. Papyrus then requests details of the related works OpenAlex returned, using OpenAlex's own identifiers for them. Titles, abstracts, notes, and the rest of your library are not sent. You can turn it off in the Similar Papers section or in Settings. OpenAlex processes these requests under its own terms; Papyrus does not control its logs or retention.

Safari extension

The optional Papyrus Import extension for Safari runs only when you invoke it — from the toolbar button, the Save Link to Papyrus context-menu item, or its keyboard shortcut. It reads the page or link you chose in order to extract citation details (title, authors, year, venue, DOI or arXiv ID) and locate a PDF, then hands that capture to the Papyrus app on your device. Papyrus may then download the paper's PDF from its source when you ask for one. The extension does not run on pages you have not invoked it on, does not collect browsing history, and sends nothing to any Papyrus server — there are none.

Apple services

Downloads, App Store account records, iCloud storage, CloudKit sync, and push delivery are handled by Apple. Apple may process data according to Apple's own policies and the Apple ID you use.

Optional Google sign-in

Google Sign-In uses Google's authentication service, not a Papyrus account server. Google documents collection of a user identifier to record authorized OAuth grants and an IP address that may estimate general location for fraud prevention. Its SDK also sends platform/version information for service operation. The SDK's privacy manifest lists name, email, phone number, coarse location, user and device identifiers, usage data, and other data categories for app functionality and/or analytics; it marks these categories as linked to the user and not used for tracking. These are SDK-level declarations, not a claim that Papyrus receives every category or that the SDK is free of analytics. Google controls its authentication, security, service diagnostics, and retention. See Google's Apple-platform SDK privacy explanation, its SDK privacy manifest, and Google's Privacy Policy.

Apple Intelligence

On a device that supports Apple Intelligence, with Apple Intelligence turned on, Papyrus uses Apple's on-device Foundation Models for:

  • Key Findings, which writes an overview and key findings from a paper's abstract, introduction, and conclusion when you choose Summarize. The result is kept in memory for display and is not saved to your library.
  • Explain Connections, which uses the titles and abstract excerpts of the papers on a Similar Papers card when you ask it to.
  • Explain Figure, which uses an image of the figure you select and a short excerpt of the surrounding page when you ask it to.
  • Concept tagging, which runs in the background on a few papers per launch (not in Low Power Mode) and stores concept, method, and dataset tags with each paper. Those tags sync like the rest of the paper's data if you use iCloud or WebDAV sync.
  • Metadata synthesis, when enrichment results are ambiguous or incomplete.

By default these features run on your device. Papyrus does not send their input to the developer and does not run its own hosted AI backend. Their input goes to another AI service only if you choose one yourself: Private Cloud Compute, or OpenAI or Anthropic with your own API key, each described below. Generated text can be wrong, and the Key Findings card says so.

Private Cloud Compute metadata refinement

Availability: this optional feature is not included in the versions of Papyrus currently on the App Store. Where a version does include it, it requires macOS 27, iOS 27, or iPadOS 27 or later, Apple Intelligence, and an eligible device, account, language, and region. If Settings says Not enabled in this build, your version does not include it. The rest of this section describes how it works in versions that do.

Private Cloud Compute (PCC) metadata refinement is separate from the on-device features above and is off by default. It fills in missing bibliographic details — such as title, authors, venue, and identifiers — from the opening pages of your papers. Before it can use Apple's cloud model, Papyrus shows a versioned consent notice in Settings → Advanced → Apple Intelligence and requires an explicit choice. Once enabled, it applies to all eligible papers in your library and can run after app launch, after importing papers, after attaching or replacing a PDF, and when you press Refine Now.

For an eligible paper, Papyrus creates an opening excerpt of extracted PDF text on your device, capped at 12,000 characters. The excerpt may include a title, author names, affiliations, abstract, journal or publisher details, identifiers, and keywords. Before routing, Papyrus:

  • removes email addresses and ORCID identifiers;
  • stops before a recognized body heading such as “Introduction” where possible;
  • keeps excerpts of 7,000 characters or fewer on your device when the on-device model is available; and
  • keeps a document on your device when it detects common confidentiality or restricted-use markings.

Detection cannot identify every sensitive document. Before enabling refinement, mark confidential, privileged, embargoed, proprietary, patient- or participant-level, or otherwise restricted papers with Keep this paper's AI processing on device. That control also blocks OpenAI metadata requests, and it syncs with the paper to your other devices.

Papyrus does not provide PCC with the PDF file, its file name or path, notes, annotations, reading activity, tags, folders, projects, library identifiers, Papyrus account identifiers, or existing metadata as separate fields.

Apple states that PCC request content is used only to fulfill the request, is not stored, and is not accessible to Apple. Apple's framework may process model, protocol, security, and service-operation metadata, limited non-content request metrics, and optional device analytics if you enabled analytics sharing with Apple. Papyrus's developer does not receive the request or its content. See Apple Intelligence & Privacy and Apple's Private Cloud Compute security guide.

Generated fields must be supported by quotes from the document, are marked with their source, and do not replace existing metadata or your edits. A failed PCC request may retry on your device.

You can turn PCC refinement off at any time. Turning it off withdraws your consent, stops new requests, and cancels queued work; a request already handed to Apple's system may finish. Turning it back on asks for consent again. When PCC is unavailable, Papyrus stays on your device or skips the model step.

Regions: Papyrus offers Private Cloud Compute only to United States App Store accounts. Everywhere else, metadata refinement and Ask This Paper run on your device only, and the PCC controls say it is not offered in your region. With no App Store account, your device's region setting decides. Apple's documentation says PCC requests are served from Apple silicon servers and from dedicated Google Cloud hardware running under the same PCC protections, and Apple does not publish where those servers are located.

Questions and digests about a paper

Availability: Ask This Paper is part of Papyrus 2.3, which is currently distributed as a TestFlight beta; the versions on the App Store do not include it yet. Its Private Cloud Compute option additionally requires macOS 27, iOS 27, or iPadOS 27 or later, Apple Intelligence, and an eligible device, account, language, and region.

Ask This Paper answers a question about the paper you have open, and Digest summarizes its method, data, results, limitations, and future work. With Compare With you can add up to two other papers from your library, and a question is then answered across them. All of this runs on Apple's on-device model by default, and that input does not leave your device through Papyrus.

You can instead choose Private Cloud Compute in the Ask This Paper panel. This is a separate optional purpose with its own versioned consent notice: consent to metadata refinement does not cover it, and neither consent depends on the other. It is used only when you submit a question or request a digest with Private Cloud Compute selected. It never runs in the background.

For each request, the content Papyrus supplies to PCC is:

  • your question, capped at 500 characters, together with your two previous questions in that conversation and the answers Papyrus showed for them (a digest sends a fixed request instead); and
  • passages from the open paper and from any papers you added with Compare With, capped at 40,000 characters in total across all of them and excluding reference lists: those Papyrus selects on your device, and any text you have selected in the PDF when you ask.

Papyrus removes email addresses and ORCID identifiers from the question and the passages. The passages are body text and may include author names, affiliations, and whatever the paper itself describes. Papyrus does not provide PCC with a paper's title as a separate field, the PDF file, its file name or path, notes, annotations, reading activity, tags, folders, projects, papers you have not added to the question, library identifiers, or Papyrus account identifiers. In a comparison the papers are labelled “Paper A”, “Paper B”, and “Paper C”.

Papers marked Keep this paper's AI processing on device and papers with detected confidentiality or restricted-use markings stay on your device, and a comparison that includes one of them is answered on your device as a whole. Detection cannot identify every sensitive document: do not choose Private Cloud Compute for confidential, privileged, embargoed, or patient- or participant-level papers.

Every point of an answer must be supported by a quote that Papyrus finds in the paper on your device; unsupported points are discarded. Answers are kept in memory until you quit the app and are not saved or synced, unless you choose Add to Notes; that saves the answer and its quotes to the paper's notes, where it is stored and synced like any note you write. If PCC cannot be used, does not respond, or a request fails, Papyrus answers on your device and says why. Generated text can be wrong; check it against the paper.

You can switch back to This Device at any time, or withdraw this consent in Settings under Private Cloud Compute. Withdrawal stops new requests; a request already handed to Apple's system may finish. Apple's handling of PCC requests is as described in the section above.

Optional OpenAI or Anthropic as your AI provider

Availability: Papyrus 2.4 and later, wherever Papyrus offers AI features. It is not offered on the mainland China App Store storefront.

In Settings → Advanced → AI Provider you can choose OpenAI (ChatGPT) or Anthropic (Claude) instead of Apple Intelligence for Ask This Paper, Digest, paper summaries, and Explain Figure. It is off by default. It needs your own API key from that company and your acceptance of a notice for that provider before anything is sent; consent to one provider does not cover the other, and neither covers Private Cloud Compute. Import, metadata, concept tagging, and Similar Papers explanations stay with Apple Intelligence.

Requests go directly from your device to the company you chose (api.openai.com or api.anthropic.com) under your own API account. Papyrus's developer does not receive your key, the requests, or the answers. Each request contains only what the feature you used needs:

  • Ask This Paper and Digest: your question, your two previous questions in that conversation and the answers Papyrus showed for them, and up to 40,000 characters of passages Papyrus selects from the open paper and from any papers you add with Compare With, including text you have selected in the PDF. The PDF file is not sent.
  • Summaries: the paper's title, abstract, and excerpts from its introduction and conclusion. This includes summaries you request through Siri or Shortcuts.
  • Explain Figure: an image of the area you select and up to 6,000 characters of that page's text.

Papyrus removes email addresses and ORCID identifiers before sending. It does not send the PDF file, its file name or path, notes, annotations, reading activity, tags, folders, projects, papers you did not include, library identifiers, or Papyrus account identifiers. The provider receives your API key to authenticate the request, and, as with any internet request, your IP address.

Papers marked Keep this paper's AI processing on device, and papers with detected confidentiality or restricted-use markings, are not sent; they are handled on your device instead. Detection cannot identify every sensitive document, and passages can contain author names and anything else the paper describes. Do not use a third-party provider for confidential, privileged, embargoed, or patient- or participant-level papers.

Answers show which provider produced them. They are kept in memory and are not saved or synced unless you choose Add to Notes. Generated text can be wrong; check it against the paper.

Each company processes requests under its own terms, retention periods, and privacy policy, which Papyrus does not control. OpenAI requests are sent with storage turned off (store: false); this is not a guarantee that nothing is retained, and both companies may keep request content for a limited time for abuse monitoring. API usage is billed by the provider to your account. See the OpenAI Privacy Policy, OpenAI API data controls, and the Anthropic Privacy Policy.

You can withdraw permission in Settings at any time. That stops new requests at once and returns these features to Apple Intelligence; a request already sent may finish. Remove Key deletes the key from this device. Neither revokes the key at the provider or deletes data the provider already holds; do that in your account with that company.

Extensions

Extensions are actions on text you select in a PDF, a note, or an abstract. They run only when you choose one. Most work entirely on your device: Copy, Add to Note, Search in Library, Remove Line Breaks, Copy as Citation, and Define, which uses the system dictionary. Translate uses Apple's system translation. Ask This Paper follows the AI provider described above.

Extensions that open a website — Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, Wikipedia, Open DOI, Open arXiv, Open Link, and any website search you add — open that site in your browser with the selected text, or the identifier found in it, as part of the address. That site then handles the request under its own policy. A Shortcut extension passes the selected text to the shortcut you named.

Extensions you write or install are stored on your device and do not sync. Before installing one, Papyrus lists what it does, including any website it opens. Script extensions are a single text-transforming function that runs on your device with no network and no file access, for at most two seconds; they cannot send your text anywhere.

Report a problem

Settings → Advanced → Report a problem opens a new issue on Papyrus's public GitHub repository in your browser, with the app version, build, and system version filled in, and copies a diagnostic report to your clipboard. Opening that page sends GitHub the prefilled version details as part of the page address; an issue is created only if you submit it, and the diagnostic report is included only if you paste it. GitHub issues are public.

Sync

Sync is optional. If you turn on iCloud Sync, Papyrus uses Apple's CloudKit service under your iCloud account to sync library records, PDFs, notes, annotations, evidence, folders, tags, projects, and sync state across devices where you enable the feature. Papyrus does not operate its own cloud backend for this data.

If you configure WebDAV sync, Papyrus connects to the WebDAV destination you provide. WebDAV sync may include the library database, PDFs, notes, evidence, sync manifests, restore points, your server URL, and your username. The WebDAV server's storage, retention, security, and access controls are governed by that server.

Your Choices

  • Use Papyrus offline.
  • Use the default library without signing in, or delete your optional Papyrus account through Settings.
  • Skip metadata enrichment when you do not want identifier lookups.
  • Leave OpenAlex suggestions in Similar Papers off, or turn them off at any time.
  • Not use the Apple Intelligence features, or turn Apple Intelligence off in your device's settings.
  • Leave the AI provider set to Apple Intelligence, withdraw permission for OpenAI or Anthropic at any time, remove your API key, or keep individual papers on device.
  • Leave Private Cloud Compute metadata refinement off, turn it off at any time, or keep individual papers on device.
  • Answer questions about a paper on your device only, by leaving This Device selected in Ask This Paper, or withdraw the Private Cloud Compute permission for questions at any time.
  • Turn individual extensions off in Settings → Extensions, and review what an extension does before installing it.
  • Leave iCloud Sync and WebDAV sync disabled.
  • Use Copy Into Library when you want Papyrus to manage PDFs, or Keep PDFs In Place when you want files to remain where they are.
  • Delete exports or shared files from their destination when you no longer need them.

Deleting items removes them from active use, but older local backups, iCloud records, or WebDAV restore points may contain historical copies until they rotate or are removed according to your settings and provider retention.

Account deletion removes app-managed backups and the associated account-scoped iCloud and configured WebDAV library. It cannot automatically remove previously exported backups, copies on other devices, provider backups, or historical unscoped sync copies outside that account's current library namespace. Remove those copies separately through their storage or provider controls.
