Abstract

Papyrus Papers is a native, local-first app for macOS and iPad that keeps your papers, PDFs, annotations, notes, and citations in one place — and turns what you read into a structured, citable argument. You import PDFs, DOIs, arXiv IDs, BibTeX, or RIS; read and mark up documents in a native reader; capture each claim with its quote, page, citation key, section role, and a confidence; then export BibTeX, RIS, CSL JSON, or Markdown for the writing tools you already use. It is a one-time purchase with no account, no subscription, and no telemetry; your library lives on your own device.

Keywords — PDF annotation · Apple Pencil · citation keys · evidence board · BibTeX / RIS / CSL JSON · Smart Collections

Introduction

Reference managers are good at storing citations and bad at storing reasoning. The sentence you actually want — “this paper supports my method, on page 3, with medium confidence” — ends up scattered across a PDF highlight, a note in another app, and a key in a .bib file that no longer remembers why it is there. Papyrus Papers closes that gap: it is one native desk where reading, annotating, and citing happen next to each other, so the evidence behind a draft stays attached to the draft.

The rest of this page is laid out the way Papyrus thinks: a worked example from a real project, Efficient Models Manuscript, shown with its actual claims and citation keys (Fig. 1–4, Table 1).

The Evidence Board

The Evidence Board is where Papyrus differs from a reference manager. A piece of evidence is not just a citation — it records the claim you are making, the quote that backs it, your own interpretation, the page, the citation key, a section role, and a confidence from 1 to 5. Evidence is grouped by seven roles — Claim, Related Work, Method, Result, Limitation, Future Work, and Rebuttal — so a project reads as an argument before you have written a word of it.

Reading & Annotation

Reading happens in a native window, not a web viewer. On both platforms you can highlight, underline, strike through, draw freehand ink, leave notes, and set bookmarks; on iPad the ink is Apple Pencil. A Markdown notes pane sits beside the page and renders LaTeX math and code, so a derivation lives next to the figure it explains. Full-text search runs across the whole library, and a reading queue keeps work-in-progress visible between sessions.3

The Library

One library, three shapes. Smart Collections are saved queries that always reflect the current library — build them from tags, journals, year ranges, type, favorites, or PDF status. Projects are working sets for the writing you are doing now, like Lit Review: Efficient Transformers; a paper can sit in several projects with its own role and evidence in each. Folders are a stable hierarchy for material with a permanent home, and they travel into project-bundle exports. Import is recursive across folders and enriches metadata from Crossref, arXiv, Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, OpenLibrary, PubMed, Zenodo, and embedded XMP, with per-field provenance when sources disagree.

Export & Citation

Everything leaves the way it came in. A single paper or a whole project exports as BibTeX, RIS, CSL JSON, Markdown, or a full bundle, and a copied citation is available in six styles. An export report flags missing PDFs, duplicate citekeys, and missing DOI, year, or pages before you hand off. The reference for vaswani2017attention, formatted six ways:

Method & Provenance

Local-first by construction. Your library, PDFs, notes, and search index live on your Mac and iPad. There is no Papyrus account and no telemetry: the app reaches the network only when you ask it to look up metadata or sync. Sync, when enabled, is your own WebDAV server, and it keeps dated restore points so a synced library can be inspected before it is restored.4 Open formats in and out mean the library is never a hostage: it is yours to verify, export, and move.
