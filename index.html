Papyrus keeps the whole research workflow beside the paper.

Native for macOS 14+ (Apple Silicon), iPadOS and iOS 17+
Version 1.6.0  ·  free on the App Store1  ·  Availability ↓
Abstract

Papyrus is a native, local-first workspace for researchers who live with PDFs over time. A paper can enter as a PDF, DOI, arXiv ID, BibTeX or RIS record, or straight from a Safari tab; become a searchable library item with editable metadata; open in a native reader; collect highlights, annotations, and Markdown + LaTeX notes; gather those notes onto themed notebooks without leaving the papers they belong to; join a project with roles and evidence; then leave as BibTeX, RIS, CSL JSON, Markdown, or a project bundle. There is no account, no subscription, and no telemetry. Your library stays on your device unless you turn on iCloud Sync or configure your own WebDAV sync.

Keywords — PDF library · Safari import · native reader · Markdown notes · Notebooks · Evidence Board · Smart Collections · iCloud / WebDAV sync · BibTeX / RIS / CSL JSON

One Library

A paper should not fragment into separate tools. The PDF, metadata, reading progress, tags, notes, project role, evidence, and citation key all belong to the same object. Papyrus keeps that object coherent from first import through the final export.

The example throughout this page follows a single Nature article: A 5.3-million-year-old deep-sea whale necropolis in the Diamantina Zone. The same record appears in the library, opens in the reader, carries Markdown notes, files those notes onto a notebook, supports a project archive, and exports through standard citation formats.

Capture & Organize

Import should produce a usable record, not a cleanup chore. Papyrus accepts PDFs, folders on Mac, DOI and arXiv identifiers, BibTeX, and RIS. It can read embedded PDF metadata, fetch scholarly metadata, and keep field-level provenance so you can see where a title, author list, year, DOI, or abstract came from when sources disagree. After a library-wide refresh, the summary lists which papers changed and which fields moved.

Safari can hand papers over directly. The bundled Safari extension captures the paper on the current tab — with its PDF when one is available — or saves any PDF, DOI, or arXiv link from the right-click (Mac) or long-press (iPad) menu. It captures from inside your browser session, so a paper behind a publisher sign-in you already have access to saves too, not just open-access files. The popup shows exactly what was captured and offers folder, tag, and project pickers so a paper lands organized instead of joining an inbox pile.

Organization has several layers because research changes shape over time. Tags describe topics. Folders keep stable archives, and a paper can live in more than one. Flags give seven colors you can name yourself, each with its own shape so they stay tellable apart at a glance. Smart Collections stay current as saved filters — several values per rule, matched on Any or All. Projects gather papers for a draft, review, or literature archive without forcing the global library to mirror a manuscript outline.

Read & Annotate

Reading happens in a native window, not a web viewer embedded in a database. You can highlight, underline, strike through, draw freehand ink, leave notes, and set bookmarks. The reader remembers progress and keeps annotations attached to the paper instead of scattering them into a separate notebook.3 Where you stopped, how far in you are, and every page you bookmarked also surface on the paper itself, in the Reading card.

Figure-heavy articles need more than a citation key. Papyrus lets the article page, the figure, the reading note, and the eventual claim remain adjacent. A Markdown notes pane renders headings, lists, tables, code, and LaTeX math, so a figure comment or draft-ready interpretation stays beside the source that prompted it.

Notebooks

Notes are written one paper at a time, but they are needed by theme. A notebook is a shelf for a topic, a chapter you are drafting, or a reading group. Filing a note onto one copies nothing and moves nothing: the note still belongs to its paper, still says which paper that is, and one click takes you there — with a way back to the shelf. A note can sit on several shelves at once, so “Optics” and “Chapter 3” never have to fight over it.

Add Notes… fills a new shelf from what you have already written, with a search field and an unfiled-only filter, so years of notes take moments rather than a note-by-note pass. A shelf can be searched by a note's text or by the paper behind it, and ordered four ways: recently edited, grouped by paper, by title, or oldest paper first — which reads your own notes forward through the literature, the way a related-work section wants to be written. Select several notes to move, unfile, or trash them together. All Notes lists everything you have written, filed or not, from your very first note.

A whole shelf exports as one Markdown document that matches what is on screen — the current order, the current search — and carries a papyrus:// link so Hookmark, Obsidian, or a plain text plan can point straight back at it.

Projects & Evidence

Projects turn a library into a working argument. A paper can belong to several projects, and each project can give that paper a different role. The Nature article can be an anchor source in a Diamantina whale-fall archive, while adjacent papers provide ecological context, dating methods, or organism-specific background.

The Evidence Board is the place where Papyrus goes beyond storing citations. Each evidence item records the claim you are making, the quote or observation that backs it, your interpretation, the page, the citation key, a section role, and a confidence score. A project starts to read like an argument before the manuscript exists.

Mac, iPad & iPhone

One app, three shapes of the same library. Papyrus is a single universal purchase-free download: a Mac window with three columns, a touch-first iPad workspace with Apple Pencil, and an iPhone companion sized for one hand. iCloud Sync keeps the same papers, notes, notebooks, highlights, flags, and reading positions current on all of them.

On iPad, the same three-column layout works under touch: read a figure page, mark it up with Apple Pencil, write the Markdown note beside it, and file that note onto a shelf. Scrolling a heavily inked PDF stays responsive — Papyrus no longer re-reads and re-saves ink for pages you only scrolled past.

On iPhone, the app is built for reading, browsing, capturing, and filing away from the desk. Continue where you left off from the home screen, read the PDF, add or edit notes, and — as of 1.6.0 — flag a paper and filter by it, create and rename notebooks, move a paper to Trash, refresh its metadata, and make folders and projects without reaching for an iPad or Mac.

Search, Sync & Privacy

A research desk has to be searchable and inspectable. Papyrus indexes titles, metadata, notes, annotations, and extracted PDF text so a phrase can lead back to the paper, page, and context where it was useful. The reading queue keeps active work visible, while filters and Smart Collections surface papers by tag, journal, year range, favorite status, flag, document type, or PDF availability.

Local-first is the default, not an add-on. Your library, PDFs, notes, annotations, search index, and project evidence live on your device. There is no Papyrus account and no telemetry. Network access is limited to actions you choose, such as metadata lookup or sync. iCloud Sync uses your own iCloud account to keep a library current across Mac, iPad, and iPhone, while WebDAV sync remains available for a server destination you control and dated restore points.4

Export & Citation

The exit path is as important as the import path. A single paper, a notebook, or a whole project can leave as BibTeX, RIS, CSL JSON, Markdown, or a full bundle. Project export can include papers, notes, evidence, citations, and a report that flags missing PDFs, duplicate cite keys, sparse metadata, or unresolved citation problems before you hand work to another tool. On Mac, a watched folder can import new PDFs on its own and a live .bib file can stay continuously updated for LaTeX and Overleaf.

Citations can also be copied in common styles without leaving the library. The reference for peng2026whaleNecropolis, formatted six ways:
